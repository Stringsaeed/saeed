Right now, I see senior engineers being pushed toward three main paths. One of them is changing fast.

## The traditional individual contributor path

Staff and principal engineer roles have always been valuable. But I think AI is changing their nature.

Deep technical judgment will still matter. The role may become less about writing every piece of architecture yourself and more about setting direction, reviewing decisions, spotting risks, and making sure the system serves the business and product.

## The product engineering path

This feels like the most promising path to me.

As AI handles more implementation, understanding the product, industry, customer, and business context becomes more valuable.

A strong product engineer does more than ship features. They understand what should be built, why it matters, and which tradeoffs to make.

I think engineers will increasingly act as orchestrators. They will guide AI tools, agents, systems, and teams toward building the right product. That sounds easier than it is. It requires taste, judgment, context, and ownership.

## The managerial path

This is only my point of view, but I have never been attracted to the managerial path. It is not less important. It simply comes with a different set of responsibilities: people decisions, politics, performance conversations, and sometimes unfair or uncomfortable tradeoffs.

Some people are great at this work and genuinely enjoy it. I do not think it fits every senior engineer.

For me, the future senior engineer looks more like a product-minded technical leader than simply the person who writes the hardest code.
